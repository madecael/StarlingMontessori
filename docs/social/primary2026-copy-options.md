# Primary Classroom 2026 — on-image copy options

On-image copy for the Primary Classroom 2026 Instagram graphics: a 9:16 hero over a warm photo of a child reaching up to top the Pink Tower, and a 1:1 dark brand card. The same copy system serves both formats.

Facts held to: Primary Classroom for children ages 3–6, opens August 2026 at Saint Mark Episcopal on Capitol Hill, twelve inaugural seats, an authentic three-year Montessori cycle including the kindergarten year, reserving tours now. Voice: gentle, generous, grown-up. Sentence case, period-ended headlines, at most one gentle exclamation and only in a CTA.

---

## The three final sets

### Set 1 — Belonging (recommended)

- **Eyebrow:** Opening August 2026
- **Headline:** A place to belong.
- **Subline:** Twelve children, one warm community, and the unhurried days of an authentic Montessori classroom.
- **CTA:** Come visit

**Why it wins:** Leads with belonging and lets "twelve children" ride in as the texture of an intimate room rather than a countdown — the exact reframe the brief asked for. "Unhurried" is the word a researching parent said she is chasing.

### Set 2 — Known

- **Eyebrow:** Primary Classroom · Ages 3–6
- **Headline:** Here, your child is known.
- **Subline:** A small Montessori classroom where guides come to know each child by name, opening this August on Capitol Hill.
- **CTA:** Schedule a tour

**Why it wins:** Names the parent's quiet fear — that their child won't be truly seen — and answers it with warmth. "Known" is the most resonant single word in the set, and "come to know each child by name" replaces the stock phrase "truly see" with something concrete.

### Set 3 — Family-run

- **Eyebrow:** Family-run on Capitol Hill
- **Headline:** Small, and family-run.
- **Subline:** Founded and guided by an AMI-trained Montessorian, where you know us and we know your child.
- **CTA:** Come visit

**Why it wins:** The most human direction — a real, trained person and a family on the other end, not an institution. Warming the headline from "Small by design." and trading the soft "Meet us" for "Come visit" keeps the relationship the parent wanted while giving the graphic a real tour-driving CTA.

---

## Recommendation

**Lead with Set 1, "A place to belong."** It is the only line both critics placed in their top three: the parent singled out "unhurried" and "belong" as the calm she is chasing, and the expert called it the strongest scarcity-as-support execution, turning "twelve" into evidence of intimacy instead of a sales hook. Run Set 2, "Here, your child is known.", as the child-led A/B alternate — equally warm, and the better fit for the 1:1 dark card where there is no reaching-child image to carry the emotion.

---

## Runner-up lines worth keeping

- **A real beginning.** (c7) — the most useful line for a parent actually researching Montessori; the three-year cycle, including the kindergarten year, answers a real practical question. Keep the subline aspirational, not comparative: "An authentic three-year Montessori cycle, including the kindergarten year, for children ages 3–6."
- **Reaching a little higher.** (c8) — the photo-native poetic cut; pairs beautifully with the child reaching for the Pink Tower. Use only where the image is unmistakable, never on the dark card, since it under-delivers on what and where on its own.
- **Soaring minds. Freeing potential.** (the tagline) — reserve as the brand or closing card. It reads as a mission statement, not an emotional hero, so it closes rather than opens.

---

## What the parent and the expert taught us

- **Lead with the child or belonging; let scarcity support.** Both critics agreed the strongest work treats "twelve" as the texture of an intimate room, not a countdown. "Room for twelve." and "Twelve seats." front the number and read as inventory to a skeptical parent.
- **Fix the warmth, do not discard it.** Where the parent loved an emotional line that the expert flagged, we kept the feeling and repaired the craft — concrete language over cliche ("know each child by name" not "truly see"), and a real tour CTA over a soft one ("Come visit" not "Meet us").
- **The headline should supply the emotion the photo cannot say.** Since the hero image already carries aspiration and the Montessori cue, the words win by naming the felt thing — known, belonging, a real human behind the school — and leaving the logistics to the subline.
